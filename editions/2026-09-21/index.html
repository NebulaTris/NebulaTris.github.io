The government put real money down

A big reason the money turned toward deeptech isn't fashion — the government started acting like a huge, patient investor, lowering risk so private VCs follow. What it committed:

₹1 lakh cr~$12B · 2025
RDI Scheme (Research, Development & Innovation)
The biggest R&D push in India's history. Cheap, long-term money for slow, hard science — AI, chips, space, biotech. Includes a Deep-Tech Fund of Funds.
₹76,000 crSemiconductor Mission
India Semiconductor Mission
Money to build chip plants and design in India, so the country stops importing almost all its chips. Total project investments already cross ₹1.6 lakh crore.
₹10,372 crIndiaAI Mission
IndiaAI Mission
National AI "compute" (thousands of GPUs), Indian AI models, and funding for AI startups — so India isn't dependent on foreign AI infrastructure.
₹10,000 crFund of Funds 1.0
Startup India Fund of Funds
The government didn't pick startups — it fed 145 VC funds, which then invested over ₹25,500 crore into 1,370+ startups. This is how it "crowds in" private money.
₹10,000 crFund of Funds 2.0 · 2025
Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0
A fresh round, pointed straight at deeptech, tech-driven manufacturing, and early-growth startups.
Why this matters (simply): deeptech takes 8–10 years and lots of cash — too scary for a normal VC alone. So the government acts like a patient anchor investor: it feeds VC funds and offers cheap loans for hard tech. That lowers everyone's risk and pulls private money in behind it.

What's coming next

The frontier: where new tech is taking things

Brand-new tech is opening markets that barely existed a few years ago. Four fronts worth watching — each with the reason it's a durable moat.

AI → the foundation

AI is now the base layer under everything. India is the world's #2 home for generative-AI startups (~890). The next wave is "agents" — AI that does tasks (books, pays, files), not just answers.

Why it's a moat: not the model (anyone can rent one) — it's owning unique data and being the rails other AI agents must plug into. Data advantage plus network effects.

Defence tech

India builds drones and weapons at home now. Via iDEX, 676 startups joined and 58 won orders worth ₹3,853 crore (~$402M). Grants run up to ₹10 crore, with no equity taken.

Why it's a moat: a customer the government legally must buy from, plus years of security clearances rivals can't rush. Guaranteed demand and a high trust barrier.

Healthtech

Past delivery apps into real medical tech: AI reads scans (Qure.ai), screens breast cancer without radiation (Niramai), detects TB from a cough on a phone (Wadhwani AI). ~30% of money now goes to devices & diagnostics.

Why it's a moat: hard-won approvals, clinical proof, and hospital workflow lock-in keep copycats years behind.

Semiconductors

India is building its first real chip fab (Tata + Taiwan's PSMC, Dholera, ~$11B), first chip ~Dec 2026. Micron already ships memory chips. 13 projects approved; ₹1,27,500 crore added for the ecosystem.

Why it's a moat: billions in capital and rare know-how — almost no one can compete head-on. In the ecosystem layer, proprietary design IP and materials chemistry.

Words, decoded

Tap any term to expand it. A comprehensive glossary — over 90 terms.

Fund & people
LP (Limited Partner)

The people or institutions — like pension funds or wealthy individuals — who put money into a VC fund but don't pick the deals themselves.

GP (General Partner)

The people who run the VC fund, decide which startups to back, and get paid to manage the LPs' money.

Carry (carried interest)

The VC fund's cut of the profits — usually around 20% — paid only if the fund actually makes money.

"2 and 20"

The classic VC fee deal: 2% of the fund charged every year to run it, plus 20% of any profit (the carry).

AIF (Alternative Investment Fund)

India's legal category for VC and private-equity funds, registered with SEBI so they can legally pool and invest money.

Fund of Funds

A fund that doesn't invest in startups directly — it invests in other VC funds, spreading its bets across many managers at once.

Dry powder

Money funds have raised but not yet invested, waiting to be deployed.

Thesis

A clear belief about the future you'd bet money on. Always has a "because."

Power law

In VC, one or two huge wins in a fund's whole portfolio can make up for every other investment losing money — returns aren't spread evenly, they're extreme.

Angel Investor

A wealthy individual who invests their own money into a very early startup, often before any VC fund gets involved.

Anchor investor

The first investor to commit to a round with enough credibility that others feel safe following them in.

Accelerator

A short, fixed-length program that gives early startups a bit of money, mentorship and a public demo day in exchange for a small slice of equity.

Incubator

A program that helps a very early idea turn into an actual company, often stepping in before there's even a product yet.

Investment Syndicate

A group of investors who pool their money together and invest in the same deal as one unit.

Capital Call

The moment a VC fund actually asks its LPs to send over money they had earlier promised to invest.

LPA (Limited Partnership Agreement)

The core legal contract between a fund's GP and its LPs that spells out how the fund will be run.

Management Fee

The yearly fee — often around 2% of the fund — a VC firm charges just to operate, no matter how the investments perform.

Fiduciary

Someone who is legally required to act in your best interest with your money, not their own.

K1

The yearly US tax form a fund sends each investor showing their personal share of the fund's profit or loss.

Key man clause

A rule letting LPs pause a fund's new investments if a specific star dealmaker at the firm leaves.

Money & rounds
Seed / Series A-B-C

The funding stages a startup climbs: Seed proves an idea works, Series A proves it can grow, Series B-C pour in bigger cheques to scale what's already working.

Lead investor

The VC who sets the price and terms of a round and puts in the biggest cheque — everyone else in the round usually follows their lead.

Term sheet

A short document laying out the price, size and rules of an investment before the full legal paperwork is signed.

Pre-money / post-money valuation

Pre-money is what the startup is worth before new cash comes in; post-money is that number plus the new money raised.

SAFE / convertible note

A simple IOU that turns into shares later, at a future round's price, instead of setting a valuation right now — used to raise money fast and cheap.

Down round

Raising money at a lower price than last time. A sign the hype cooled.

TAM

Total size of the market if you won everything. VCs need this to be huge.

Product-market fit

The moment a product finally clicks with customers — demand pulls it forward instead of the founder having to push it.

Due diligence

The homework a VC does before investing — checking the numbers, the team, the contracts and the claims are all real.

Moat

What stops rivals from copying you: special data, hard tech, loyal customers, a licence.

Exit

How a VC turns its stake back into cash: the startup gets bought, or goes public (IPO).

Unicorn / soonicorn

A unicorn is a startup worth $1 billion or more; a soonicorn is one investors think is close to getting there.

Secondary

Buying shares from an existing shareholder (an early investor or employee) instead of the company itself — no new money reaches the startup.

IPO & M&A

The two main ways a startup "exits": IPO lists its shares on a public stock market; M&A means a bigger company buys it outright.

409A Valuation

An independent, IRS-recognised appraisal of what a private company's common stock is actually worth, mainly used to price employee stock options fairly.

Allocation

The amount of a funding round an investor is actually permitted to put money into, which is sometimes less than they wanted.

Bootstrapping

Growing a company using only its own revenue or the founders' own savings, without taking outside investor money.

Bridge Loan / Financing

Short-term money that carries a startup from where it is now until its next real funding round closes.

Convertible Debt

A loan that's built to turn into equity later, usually at a discount, instead of simply being paid back in cash.

Convertible

Any investment that's designed to convert into equity shares down the line rather than pricing the company today.

Fair Market Value

What a share, or the whole company, would honestly sell for right now between a willing buyer and a willing seller.

Friends and Family Round

The very first, informal money a founder raises from people who trust them personally, before any professional investor gets involved.

Fully Diluted

A company's total share count if every option, warrant and convertible security were already turned into actual stock.

Growth Equity

Money invested into an already-proven company to help it scale faster, sitting between early-stage VC and buyout-stage private equity.

Inside Round

A funding round where only a startup's existing investors put in money, with no new outside investor joining.

Letter of Intent (LOI)

A short, mostly non-binding document that lays out the basic shape of a deal before the full contract gets written.

Liquidity

How easily an asset — like startup shares — can actually be turned into spendable cash.

Elevator pitch

A pitch for your startup made so short and clear you could deliver the whole thing during one elevator ride.

Legal & rights
Cap table

The spreadsheet showing who owns what percentage of a startup — founders, employees, and every investor.

Dilution

Your ownership percentage shrinking because the company issued new shares to new investors — the pie has more slices now.

ESOP

Employee Stock Ownership Plan — company shares set aside so employees can own a piece of what they're building, usually earned over time.

Vesting

The schedule that makes you earn your shares or options gradually over time (often 4 years), instead of getting them all on day one.

Accredited Investor

Someone who meets certain income or wealth rules that legally allow them to invest in private deals not open to the general public.

Anti-dilution Clause

A protection that adjusts an early investor's price if a later round sells shares more cheaply, so their stake isn't unfairly watered down.

Blended Preferences

When investors from several different funding rounds share one combined payout priority instead of being paid out strictly in the order they invested.

Clawback

A rule letting a fund take back money it already paid its GPs if the fund's overall results later don't justify it.

Cliff

The waiting period before any of your vesting shares actually start becoming yours — leave before it ends and you get nothing.

Come Along Rights

The right for a smaller shareholder to join in and sell their shares on the same terms when a bigger shareholder sells theirs.

Control Rights

Contract terms giving an investor a real say over specific big decisions a company makes, beyond just owning shares.

Common Stock

The everyday type of company share, usually held by founders and employees, that pays out only after preferred shareholders are covered.

Covenant

A promise a company makes to its investors or lenders — like keeping certain numbers healthy — as a condition of the deal.

Cumulative Dividend

A dividend that keeps piling up unpaid each year until the company finally pays it, instead of just disappearing if skipped.

Cutback Rights

A company's right to shrink how many shares its existing investors can sell in an IPO, so new public investors get enough to buy.

Demand Registration Rights

The right for investors to force a company to file the paperwork needed to sell their shares publicly.

Drag-Along Rights

The right letting a majority of shareholders force everyone else to sell too when the company gets acquired.

Exercise Price (Strike Price)

The fixed price you pay to actually buy your shares when you use, or "exercise," a stock option.

First Refusal Rights

The right to match any outside offer and buy shares yourself before they can be sold to someone else.

Full Ratchet

The toughest kind of anti-dilution protection — it resets an early investor's price all the way down to match a later, cheaper round.

Grandfather Rights

Letting an existing investor, or an old rule, keep more favourable terms even after the terms change for everyone new.

General Solicitation

Publicly advertising that you're raising money — something normally restricted to protect everyday, non-professional investors.

Information Rights

An investor's contractual right to regularly see a startup's financials and key numbers after they've invested.

Investor's Rights Agreement

The master contract that bundles an investor's information, voting and other protections into one document.

Issuer

Simply the company that is selling the shares or securities in a given deal.

JOBS Act

A US law that made it easier for smaller companies to raise money and for everyday people to invest in startups, including via crowdfunding.

Liquidation

Shutting a company down and selling off whatever is left to pay back whoever is owed money, in a set order of priority.

Liquidation Preference

A rule guaranteeing certain investors get their money back first — sometimes with a multiple on top — before anyone else gets paid in a sale.

Lock-up Period

A set window after an IPO during which company insiders are contractually blocked from selling their shares.

Metrics
Burn rate & runway

Burn rate is how fast a startup spends its cash each month; runway is how many months are left before the money runs out.

ARR / MRR

Annual / Monthly Recurring Revenue — the predictable subscription income a company can count on every year or month.

CAC & LTV

CAC is what it costs to win one customer; LTV is how much that customer is worth over their whole relationship with you. LTV should beat CAC by a lot.

NRR (net revenue retention)

Of the revenue you had from existing customers a year ago, how much is left today after upgrades, downgrades and cancellations — above 100% means existing customers alone are growing the business.

Churn

The share of customers (or revenue) that leaves in a given period. Lower is better — it means people are sticking around.

GMV

Gross Merchandise Value — the total value of everything sold through a platform, before the platform takes its cut.

Traction

Early, real proof that people want what you built — users, revenue, or usage actually going up.

Unit economics

Does one customer make money after what it cost to serve them?

ARPU

The average amount of revenue a single user brings in over a given period.

AOV

The average amount of money a customer spends in a single order or transaction.

ARRG Ratio

A quick check on growth efficiency — how much new recurring revenue a company added compared with what it spent to get it.

Conversion Rate or Ratio

The share of people who take the action you wanted, like buying, out of everyone who had the chance to.

Exit Velocity

How fast a startup looks to be accelerating toward a big outcome, like an IPO or acquisition, based on its growth trend.

Earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT)

A company's profit before interest and tax costs are subtracted, used as a rough read on how the core business is performing.

Gross Margin

What's left over from a sale after covering the direct cost of delivering it, before any other expenses are paid.

Deeptech & India context
Fab

Short for "fabrication plant." A giant, spotlessly clean factory that makes chips out of silicon, almost atom by atom. Costs billions and years to build.

Micron / PSMC / ASML

Micron: a US memory-chip firm now packaging/testing chips in Gujarat. PSMC: Tata's Taiwanese partner bringing chipmaking know-how. ASML: the Dutch firm that makes the only machines advanced chips can't be built without.

Node (28nm, 40nm…)

How tiny a chip's features are. Smaller = more advanced and pricier. India starts with larger "mature" nodes, which are cheaper and power most everyday electronics.

Wadhwani AI

An Indian non-profit lab building AI for social good. Its "Cough Against TB" tool screens for tuberculosis from a cough recorded on a normal phone.

iDEX

A government program that gives defence-tech startups grants (up to ₹10 crore, no equity) and a path to sell directly to the armed forces.

AI agent

AI that doesn't just answer but does things for you — books a trip, pays a bill, files a form. The next wave, and it needs new "rails" built for it.
