The backstory

How India got here

To see where the money is going, it helps to see the road behind it. India's startup story came in waves — each teaching a lesson the next built on.

1990s – 2000s

The "back office of the world"

India got famous selling software services — Infosys, TCS, Wipro doing work for foreign firms. Brilliant engineers, but selling time, not products. This built the talent, not yet the startups.

2008 – 2020

The consumer internet boom

After the 2008 crash, VCs poured money into apps for Indian shoppers and riders — Flipkart, Ola, Myntra. Starting up stopped being taboo. This is where "grow fast, lose money, worry later" was born.

2015 – 2021

UPI, SaaS and the edtech bubble

UPI made payments almost free, so fintech exploded. SaaS firms sold to the world. COVID pumped huge money into edtech — a bubble about to pop.

2022 – 2023

The funding winter

Global money got expensive, investment dried up, and poster-child Byju's collapsed. Valuations were slashed. A hard reminder that a business must actually make money.

2024 – today

The deeptech turn

Money now moves to hard tech — chips, space, defence, AI, medical devices — and to disciplined, profitable firms. India crossed 2 lakh startups and 132 unicorns, and founders build "from India, for the world."

Why the shift is happening

The government put real money down

A big reason the money turned toward deeptech isn't fashion — the government started acting like a huge, patient investor, lowering risk so private VCs follow. What it committed:

₹1 lakh cr~$12B · 2025
RDI Scheme (Research, Development & Innovation)
The biggest R&D push in India's history. Cheap, long-term money for slow, hard science — AI, chips, space, biotech. Includes a Deep-Tech Fund of Funds.
₹76,000 crSemiconductor Mission
India Semiconductor Mission
Money to build chip plants and design in India, so the country stops importing almost all its chips. Total project investments already cross ₹1.6 lakh crore.
₹10,372 crIndiaAI Mission
IndiaAI Mission
National AI "compute" (thousands of GPUs), Indian AI models, and funding for AI startups — so India isn't dependent on foreign AI infrastructure.
₹10,000 crFund of Funds 1.0
Startup India Fund of Funds
The government didn't pick startups — it fed 145 VC funds, which then invested over ₹25,500 crore into 1,370+ startups. This is how it "crowds in" private money.
₹10,000 crFund of Funds 2.0 · 2025
Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0
A fresh round, pointed straight at deeptech, tech-driven manufacturing, and early-growth startups.
Why this matters (simply): deeptech takes 8–10 years and lots of cash — too scary for a normal VC alone. So the government acts like a patient anchor investor: it feeds VC funds and offers cheap loans for hard tech. That lowers everyone's risk and pulls private money in behind it.

What's coming next

The frontier: where new tech is taking things

Brand-new tech is opening markets that barely existed a few years ago. Four fronts worth watching — each with the reason it's a durable moat.

AI → the foundation

AI is now the base layer under everything. India is the world's #2 home for generative-AI startups (~890). The next wave is "agents" — AI that does tasks (books, pays, files), not just answers.

Why it's a moat: not the model (anyone can rent one) — it's owning unique data and being the rails other AI agents must plug into. Data advantage plus network effects.

Defence tech

India builds drones and weapons at home now. Via iDEX, 676 startups joined and 58 won orders worth ₹3,853 crore (~$402M). Grants run up to ₹10 crore, with no equity taken.

Why it's a moat: a customer the government legally must buy from, plus years of security clearances rivals can't rush. Guaranteed demand and a high trust barrier.

Healthtech

Past delivery apps into real medical tech: AI reads scans (Qure.ai), screens breast cancer without radiation (Niramai), detects TB from a cough on a phone (Wadhwani AI). ~30% of money now goes to devices & diagnostics.

Why it's a moat: hard-won approvals, clinical proof, and hospital workflow lock-in keep copycats years behind.

Semiconductors

India is building its first real chip fab (Tata + Taiwan's PSMC, Dholera, ~$11B), first chip ~Dec 2026. Micron already ships memory chips. 13 projects approved; ₹1,27,500 crore added for the ecosystem.

Why it's a moat: billions in capital and rare know-how — almost no one can compete head-on. In the ecosystem layer, proprietary design IP and materials chemistry.

Cheat sheet

Words, decoded

Tap any term to expand it.

Thesis

A clear belief about the future you'd bet money on. Always has a "because."

Moat

What stops rivals from copying you: special data, hard tech, loyal customers, a licence.

TAM

Total size of the market if you won everything. VCs need this to be huge.

Unit economics

Does one customer make money after what it cost to serve them?

Exit

How a VC turns its stake back into cash: the startup gets bought, or goes public (IPO).

Down round

Raising money at a lower price than last time. A sign the hype cooled.

Dry powder

Money funds have raised but not yet invested, waiting to be deployed.

Fab

Short for "fabrication plant." A giant, spotlessly clean factory that makes chips out of silicon, almost atom by atom. Costs billions and years to build.

Micron / PSMC / ASML

Micron: a US memory-chip firm now packaging/testing chips in Gujarat. PSMC: Tata's Taiwanese partner bringing chipmaking know-how. ASML: the Dutch firm that makes the only machines advanced chips can't be built without.

Node (28nm, 40nm…)

How tiny a chip's features are. Smaller = more advanced and pricier. India starts with larger "mature" nodes, which are cheaper and power most everyday electronics.

Wadhwani AI

An Indian non-profit lab building AI for social good. Its "Cough Against TB" tool screens for tuberculosis from a cough recorded on a normal phone.

iDEX

A government program that gives defence-tech startups grants (up to ₹10 crore, no equity) and a path to sell directly to the armed forces.

AI agent

AI that doesn't just answer but does things for you — books a trip, pays a bill, files a form. The next wave, and it needs new "rails" built for it.
